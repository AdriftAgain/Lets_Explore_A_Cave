Classical Karst

Location: Slovenia

Known for: Paleolithic occupation, development of karstology and speleology

A karst is a landscape with many depressive geographical features. It is created when bedrock is dissolved, which forms sinkholes, sinking streams, springs, and (most relevantly) caves.

The Classical Karst is about 6,400km2 with over 6,000 known caves. It has existed since the Triassic era and been home to humans since the Palaeolithic, giving it a rich history both geologically and anthropologically.

Aside from being pivotal to scientific research, the Classical Karst is also known for the development of cave tourism. One of its caves, Vilenica, has records of tourism from 1633, during the Baroque period. Tourism gradually developed, becoming more organized and intense during the 19th century and still remains strong today.

The Classical Karst is also widely considered the birthplace of karstology and speleology, with the study beginning around the end of the 16th century, during the early Renaissance. In modern day, the Karst Research Institute was formed and still holds a leading role in speleology.
