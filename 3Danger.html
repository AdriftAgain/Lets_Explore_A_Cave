Cave Dangers

While there are no active threats, like wild beasts or monsters in the dark, you will find plenty of dangers when you descend into the maw of a cave.

Darkness

Cities never sleep at night and even the most remote surfaces on earth have the moon shining down. But in a cave, there is nothing. It is truly dark. This poses two risks to us creatures who are used to the light: inability to navigate, which can lead to injuries from the environment or getting lost, and negative effects on the mind, since darkness is a natural fear and being in it for too long can cause insanity.

The Cold

As caves do not see the sun, they do not get warmed by it. They hover around 10-15°C, but can become colder the more northern they are. As a result, unprepared cavers can develop hypothermia, which quickly becomes deadly.

Flooding

During rainy weather, water can enter the cave, making this hostile environment even more hazardous. In a trickle, the ground becomes wet and slippery, making passages harder to traverse and falls more likely. In a roar, caves can quickly flood. While the entire cavern may not be underwater, some passages essential to travel may be, making escape dangerous. Cavers who did not check the weather before leaving can unexpectedly become trapped without any way to call for help.

Rocks

Most cave injuries are caused by rocks, since the environment is made up of them. Sharp rocks can cause cuts, loose rocks can fall and hit or trap you, the unevenness of the floor can cause falls, unexpectedly low cave ceilings can hurt your head. Small cuts and bruises can be mended easily, but other more dire injuries, like concussions or broken bones, require more intense care. It is important that a caver is always aware of the environment around them.

Exhaustion

Caving may seem easy, but it is physically taxing and you may bite off more than you can chew. People often won’t realize they are tired until they are too tired to continue, making getting out of the cave harder.

Bad Mental State

A cave is dark and claustrophobic, already difficult to cope with. But if you have taken a mind-altering substance, haven’t slept, haven’t eaten properly, or are even in an emotional state, then it will be even worse. While caving, it is important you are level headed so you are at less risk of injury or a mental break.
