How to Explore

The hobby or practice of exploring caves.

Cav-ing

/ˈkāviNG/

The sport of exploring caves.

Spelunking and caving are often interchangeable terms, at least to those unfamiliar with the hobby. But in the community, they are mainly differentiated by the level of expertise involved. ‘Caving’ is often thought of as cave exploration that is more sophisticated– requiring experience, knowledge, and advanced techniques. Whereas ‘spelunking’ is more casual, associated with beginners and amateurs. There’s even some stigma around being described as a ‘spelunker’, as it implies being untrained and unknowledgeable, and having less respect for the cave.

Cave Diving

Underwater diving in water-filled caves. It may be done as an extreme sport, a way of exploring flooded caves for science, search and rescue.

Cave diving is the exploration of underwater caves, typically using diving equipment. It is an extreme variation of caving and/or diving, since it requires such advanced skills and comes with more dangerous risks. Because of the cost of training and proper equipment, cave diving is not as widespread a hobby as caving.

Speleology

/spē-lē-ˈä-lə-jē/

The scientific study or exploration of caves

Speleology is the scientific side of caving. While cavers enjoy the community building or physical activity aspects of exploring the underground, speleology studies it– everything from isolated cave ecosystems, mineral makeup of stalagmites or stalactites, analysis of cave features, and plenty more.

How to start caving?

The easiest way is to just go into a cave. However, this is dangerous since caving comes with many risks you might not even be aware of. You could harm yourself, or even harm the cave. It is recommended to get experience first, lest you end up another caving horror story on youtube.

One way to start is to hire a guide. As professionals, they are required to be experienced and knowledgeable. They will be able to coach you on safety measures and techniques, often be intimately familiar with specific caves, and will be able to administer first aid should anything happen. The only caveat is that this can be very expensive.

For a cheaper option, you can look to your local caving community. Wherever there is a cave, there will likely be a club. As hobbyists themselves, they’ll be welcoming and enthusiastic! They’ll know the best places to get equipment and be familiar with local caves. There will be a mix of both people who have experience, who can help guide you, and people who are just beginning, so you can learn together.
