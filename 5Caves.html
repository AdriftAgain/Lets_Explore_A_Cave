Minimum Impact Caving

An important part of caving is respecting the caves you explore. Since they are such isolated environments, they have delicate ecosystems that can be completely overturned by a single error. Here are some guidelines to follow to ensure minimum impact:

Wash all your gear after and before going into a cave. This prevents transfer of microbes between different caves. In North America, this is especially important because of White Nose Syndrome, a disease which is decimating bat populations.

Avoid damaging the rock by moving carefully, using footwear with non-marring/non-marking soles, and using soft/padded cave packs.

Do not smoke or light a campfire, as the smoke will affect the air composition.

Do not disturb cave dwelling organisms. For larger animals, avoid waking bats and killing insects. For microbes, avoid touching the rock as much as possible and do not interact with pools of water. Move as carefully as possible.

Limit introduction of organic material. This includes human waste. If you must go to the bathroom or vomit, you must do so in a container and take it out with you. Do not spit and avoid scratching skin/hair, which will cause it to drop into the cave. When you eat or drink, do so with a container below to catch any falling food particles or liquid.

Follow the leave no trace policy. Take nothing and leave nothing.

Do not share cave information publicly, since ignorant or disrespectful parties may take advantage of the knowledge.
