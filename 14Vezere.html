Caves of the Vézère Valley

Location: Southwestern France

Known for: Paleolithic occupation

The Caves of the Vézère Valley were home to humans from over 400,000 years ago to about 8,000 years ago. In 1979, UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site due to ethnological, anthropological, and aesthetic reasons.

With a territory roughly 500km2, they contain a vast amount of relics from the Paleolithic era, with over 150 sites of prehistoric occupation and 30 caves with prehistoric art, connecting us to humans from thousands of years ago. Ancient living areas give us insight into their daily lives, art shows how they interpret their world, funerary places tell us how they grieved their dead– we are reminded that they are not just a paragraph in a history book, but were just like us. Because of the length of time that the caves were occupied, we get to see how humankind evolved. We can see improvements to tools and innovations that led up to our modern technologies and see the changing landscape through their art. And because of the vast area the caves occupy, we also see the diversity of prehistoric societies and their interactions with one another.

There is so much to discover about ourselves and our past, all just buried within reach underground.
