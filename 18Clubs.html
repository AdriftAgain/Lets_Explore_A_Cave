Formalization of Caving

The exploration of caves as a hobby, not as survival, started to form around the 17th century, with records in 1633 telling of cave tourism in the Classical Karst’s Vilencia cave. And in the 19th century, it began to become more popular.

The Yorkshire Rambler’s Club was founded in 1892 and is considered one of the oldest established caving clubs in the world. While initially proposed as a club for mountaineering, the number of founders who enjoyed cave exploration eventually influenced it to include some caving as well.

More clubs followed in the decades after, with the British Speleological Association forming in 1935 and the National Speleological Society in 1941. These clubs were foundational for caving, as they provided much needed organization and an avenue for anyone interested in beginning. For caving as a hobby, they formalized safety by developing guidelines, training, and equipment, providing an open and helpful community, and promoting respect and knowledge of caves. For speleology, they help keep records of caves and maps, along with being able to put scientists in contact with experienced cavers. They also work with emergency response teams, with experienced cavers sometimes helping to rescue people who have gotten stuck in caves.
