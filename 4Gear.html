Gear

It is incredibly important to go caving with the right equipment, to lessen risk of injury/death and just generally make the experience more enjoyable. It is a lot more fun to cave while in warm clothes than to be walking in darkness and shivering from the cold.

Emergency Kit: Essential for general first aid and genuine emergencies. Contains medical gear, repair tools, backup items, and more.

Packs: Bags used to carry everything, specially made to withstand physical damage. Often waterproof for wet caves and equipped with clips or buckles as opposed to zippers.

Flashlights: Provides light so you can actually see in the otherwise pitch black cave. Typically cavers have one strapped to their helmet and another handheld.

Helmets: Lessens any potential head injuries from you falling, rocks falling, or collisions with cave wall/ceiling. Often paired with a strap-on flashlight.

Gloves: Protects hands from injury and the cold, as well as provides grip

Knee and Elbow pads: Lessens any potential injuries to knees and elbows, especially since you must often crawl through a cave.

Shoes: Must be sturdy and closed-toe to prevent injury and so they are able to be used with harnesses and ropes.

Harnesses and Ropes: Used to scale difficult vertical terrain and as a safety measure against falling.

Ascenders and Descenders: Devices that make gripping rope easier

Knives: Used to cut up food or cut up rope.

Extra batteries: Often brought for the flashlights, but can be used for other things. It is very important to be prepared for either a battery malfunction or an unexpectedly longer caving session.

Food and drink: It is important to stay hydrated and have proper nutrition when doing such a physically taxing activity.
