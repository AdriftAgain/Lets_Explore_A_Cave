Emily Davis Mobley

March 30 - April 4 1991, Lechuguilla Cave, New Mexico, USA

On Saturday March 30 1991 at 1:00pm, 40-year-old experienced cave explorer, Emily Davis Mobley, entered Lechuguilla Cave with four other teammates with the goal of mapping the unexplored section of the cave’s Fubar Passage. Due to Park Service regulations, they were given a 24 hour time limit on their trip.

After having been in the cave for about 11 hours, the group started to believe they would not be able to complete their mission in time. Instead, they pivoted to exploring a different section so that they did not make the trip for nothing.

Sixteen and a half hours into their trip, the group was climbing back up a breakdown when a rock Emily Davis Mobley had been using as a handhold came loose. She slid back down the 12-foot slope and the rock, which was about 30-35kg, struck her leg– she suffered a fracture. The rest of her teammates delivered first aid and carried her to a more stable section of the cave, while one of them travelled back through the cave. He eventually came across another exploration party and informed them of the situation. One went to the surface to notify rescue, the rest continued on their exploration, and Mobley’s teammate went back to her.

To rescue Mobley, complicated contraptions made of ropes and pulleys were used to lift her through vertical sections. She emerged from the cave at 2:30am Thursday April 4th to grateful family, relieved rescuers, and 31 delicious pizzas.

While Mobley claims that fatigue did not cause this incident, some have placed blame on the regulations Park Service had made. In an effort to allow the large number of cavers wanting to enter, parties were given only 24 hours to be in the cave. This could have caused Mobley’s party to feel stressed which, combined with the lack of a natural and comfortable sleep cycle in a cave, could have caused them to not be as careful.

This incident is a reminder of the importance of having a proper mental state when caving.
