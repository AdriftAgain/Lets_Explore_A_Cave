Nutty Putty Cave

Nov 25 2009. Nutty Putty Cave, Utah, USA

On November 24 2009, John Jones and some of his family entered the Nutty Putty Cave to casually tour the entrance. After exploring for a bit, some of the party left while others continued to explore, including John. This led to them travelling down a tight passage that required them to belly-crawl. When the passage turned downwards, John continued to crawl through. Eventually, he realized he could not shimmy his way back up and had no room to turn around. He told the others to go back, while he continued down, trying to find a place to turn around. He did not find it and yelled back up to his family that he was stuck.

At 10:00pm, his family alerted emergency services and a rescue effort was quickly assembled. An experienced caver, Rob Stillmar, made an attempt to free John, but was unable to. Since he would also have to crawl into the passage head-first, straps were tied around his legs so that he could be pulled out. When John was unable to be freed, straps were also tied around his legs.

At 8:00AM the next day, 12 hours since John had entered the cave, he was still stuck. Rescuers were setting up a haul system in an attempt to pull John free. Initially it seemed to work, bringing him up a few feet, but due to the twists and turns of the cavern, it failed. This knocked a r escuer unconscious and dropped John down back into the passage. An hour later, John became unresponsive and rescue efforts hastened.

Unfortunately, at 10:00pm November 25 2009, John Jones died. It was declared too dangerous to retrieve his body. The Nutty Putty Cave was sealed and still remains closed to this day.

This tragedy reminds us of how important caution and knowledge is when caving. John Jones’s inexperience led to him making a poor decision that sadly cost him his life.
