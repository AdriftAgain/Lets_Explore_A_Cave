Binkley Cave Rescue

Dec 20 2016. Binkley Cave, Indiana, USA

On December 18 2016, seven experienced cavers entered the Binkley Cave System to survey a section of the cave that would take a couple hours to reach. They entered in the afternoon and said they would be out by 3:00am.

While it was raining that day, the forecast predicted less than an inch of rain, so the group determined it safe enough to continue.

Partially through their journey, the group encountered a passage that was filled with water, with only a little space above for air. One caver was not comfortable with continuing, and so the group began to discuss splitting into two: one to go back and the other to continue. However, they noticed that the passage’s water level was increasing. Unbeknownst to them, the rain had increased, with more than 2 inches now having fallen.

The entire group began to head back to the surface, but were unable to reach it. Due to the storm, a river in the cave had increased in strength and was no longer safe to cross. The group tried to wait out the storm, but were still trapped underground at 6:00am, past the time they had said they’d be out.

Luckily, on the surface, they were being looked out for. The increase in rainfall had been noticed and a local caver, Anmar Mirza, was contacted. He ordered for the cave’s water levels to be monitored and notified other cavers to be ready, in case a rescue was needed.

By 6:00am, the rain had stopped, but the river’s water level was 4-5 above the usual.

By 9:30am, Anmar Mirza contacted the Indiana Department of Natural Resources to officially start a rescue. ATwo teams were organized– the first was made up of three people who were familiar with the cave, the second was made up of two people who were strong cavers but not as familiar. They would enter together, with Team One leading the way to the section they theorized the trapped cavers to be. Once the trapped cavers were located, Team Two would head back to report their findings to guide the next steps of the rescue.

At 10:50pm December 19, Team Two came back to the surface, reporting that they had found the trapped cavers alive and well. An evacuation team was sent in and all the cavers made it back to the surface by 3:00am December 20 2019.

Rainy weather can make caves dangerous, as seen in this incident. Especially in long cave systems, which have more sections that are likely to become flooded and take hours to travel. Even with the forecast only predicting less than an inch of rain, it is important to remember that conditions can change rapidly and so if you decide to continue, you must be aware and prepared, like this group.

Additionally, this incident reminds us of the importance of communication. The group let someone know that they would be caving, where they were going, and what time to expect them out. If they hadn’t, no one would know to rescue them. The landowner was aware of the dangers rain posed to cavers and because of that was able to warn the appropriate people. And the government was able to collaborate with other cavers to plan and carry out the rescue. Without these interactions going so smoothly, the outcome of this rescue could have been very different.
